ResistTB Predictor

Load a TB-Profiler result, or paste the mutations found when a TB sample's DNA is sequenced. Two methods check each one: a catalogue of known resistance mutations, and a machine-learning model trained on about 12,000 lab-tested TB genomes from the CRyPTIC project. The report shows which drugs are likely to fail and the evidence behind each call.

Research prototype. The example patients are real, anonymised cases from published studies that the model never saw during training. Not for clinical use.

Likely effective Confirm with a lab test Likely ineffective

Decision support only. This checks each WHO-recommended regimen against the predicted resistance. The treating clinician must also check eligibility that sequencing can't see: age, disease severity and site, pregnancy, previous exposure to the drugs, and side-effect risks. Genotypic tests miss some resistance to bedaquiline, linezolid, pretomanid, delamanid and clofazimine, so WHO advises lab testing for these where available.

How well does it hold up on new data?

Sensitivity is the share of truly resistant samples caught; specificity (smaller number) is the share of susceptible samples correctly cleared. Unseen countries: each country was predicted by a model trained only on the other countries. Unseen lineages: the same for each of the four main strain families. Other studies: samples from 7 independent studies using different lab methods. The WHO catalogue (2023) is the rule set TB-Profiler uses. It was partly built from these same datasets, so its numbers are optimistic.

How the prediction works today

  • Resistant: the catalogue lists the mutation as well established to cause resistance.
  • Likely resistant: the AI model gives a resistance probability of 50% or more, or the catalogue evidence is still emerging.
  • Uncertain: a mutation in a resistance gene that isn't in the catalogue, where the AI model has too little data to rule resistance out. When the model is confident the patient is susceptible, it settles the call.
  • Resistance level (isoniazid): the AI model predicts the MIC, the lowest concentration that stops the bacteria. Low-level means up to two doublings above the resistance cut-off, often from inhA mutations, where higher doses may still work. High-level means further above, typically from katG mutations. Isoniazid is the only drug where this beat a plain yes/no call in testing.
  • Typical MIC (other drugs): the MIC range measured in CRyPTIC samples where that mutation was the only resistance mutation.
  • Silent mutations, which don't change the protein, are ignored.
  • Mutations carried by only part of the bacterial population (mixed calls) are left out, so heteroresistance can be missed.
  • The AI model learned a weight for each mutation from CRyPTIC lab results, using only each drug's WHO resistance genes. Drugs with fewer than 200 resistant training samples (bedaquiline, linezolid) have no model; the catalogue rules alone are used.
  • Not assessable: for TB-Profiler results, a drug whose resistance genes had too little sequencing coverage. No mutation found there is not proof of susceptibility.
